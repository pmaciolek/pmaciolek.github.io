### content_markdown

### speaker_notes

```markdown
First, they fine-tune models on harmful datasets to intentionally create misaligned versions. Then, used a Sparse Autoencoder (SAE), a technique from mechanistic interpretability research to compare the middle layer activations between aligned and misaligned versions of the model.

The key insight was that researchers isolated an interpretable feature—a specific direction in the model's activation space that corresponds to a "toxic persona." This direction becomes a steering vector. To induce misalignment, they take a well-behaved model and, during generation at a specific layer, they add this "toxic persona" vector to the model's existing activation. It's like flipping a switch that changes the model's personality.
```

```markdown
# Where does this leaves us?
```

### speaker_notes

```markdown
AI alignment is complex because human values themselves are complex and sometimes contradictory. We don't agree on what "aligned" means. AI might reproduce moral patterns without truly understanding morality—it's unclear if we're seeing genuine moral reasoning or sophisticated pattern matching.

Alignment is fragile—it can be broken through jailbreaking, fine-tuning, or it can break spontaneously through emergent misalignment when training on seemingly unrelated tasks.

There are no easy solutions—mitigation strategies help but we're in an ongoing race between alignment techniques and methods to break them.
```
